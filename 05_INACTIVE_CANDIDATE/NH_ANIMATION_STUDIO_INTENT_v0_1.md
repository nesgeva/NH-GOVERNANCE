## The concept, in Ness's own terms

1. **N.H makes animation work itself:** 2D animations, 3D animations,
   icons and drawings, and 2D worlds alongside the 3D world. (Ness's
   dictated words were "2D walls ... other than 3D walls"; Claude's
   reading, stated in chat and not objected to, is "2D worlds alongside
   the 3D world".)

2. **N.H operates an animation program itself** (for example Blender),
   rather than producing video with AI video generators. Ness chose this
   route outright ("Option 1 100%"). The specific program is not decided.

3. **The look:** 2D anime with a consistent animation style. Animation,
   characters and backgrounds are 2D; only effects are 3D.

4. **N.H does a lot of the work:** it makes drafts and animations that Ness
   can present to studios, and that studios can begin working on top of
   or use as reference.

5. **Later, inside Ness's apps:** N.H brought into his PC and his apps
   (Adobe or similar) to help him use them better. Ness ranked this after
   the animation work.

## Points raised in the discussion (Claude, 2026-10-01) — not decided

Recorded so whoever designs this later starts from them. None is a
decision; each is Ness's to accept, change or drop.

- **Existing bridges (web research, 2026-10-01; not verified locally):**
  connectors that let an AI drive creative programs already exist. Blender
  has one that works through its own scripting; Unreal has several; one
  combined server drives Blender and Unreal together. Adobe runs an
  official one (generally available since April 2026) on Adobe's own
  servers, which would take work off Ness's PC; community-made ones run
  locally for After Effects, Illustrator and Photoshop.
- **One program or two:** Blender does 2D animation (its Grease Pencil
  tools), 3D effects and final compositing in one place, and is the
  program an AI can control best today. Dedicated 2D programs (OpenToonz,
  Toon Boom Harmony) are stronger for pure 2D but much harder for an AI to
  drive, and would need a second bridge.
- **Depth without 3D backgrounds:** flat 2D layers placed at different
  distances give real camera depth while everything stays 2D (the classic
  "multiplane camera").
- **3D effects that match the 2D look:** flat anime colors, drawn outlines,
  and the same frame rhythm as the drawings (animated "on twos" if the
  drawings are), so the effects don't look smoother than the animation.
- **Consistency as N.H's job:** style rules per project (character sheets,
  color palettes, line rules, timing rules, effect rules) held as memories
  linked to that project. N.H checks its own renders against them, using
  the planned image-understanding describer.
- **Studio-friendly output:** every element on its own layer (characters,
  backgrounds, effects); image sequences and video; storyboard files;
  anime timing sheets; editable project files; standard sizes (1080p, 24
  frames per second).
- **Parts it would touch:** the model layer (planning the steps), the
  creation store (projects, versions, renders), the image-understanding
  describer, Ness's interface and approval gates, and §0B logging. The
  logs would also record who made what, Ness or N.H.
- **Graphics cards:** both of Ness's cards are planned for N.H's models,
  but Blender also needs a card to render, so a sharing rule is needed
  (render while the models are idle, render on the processor, or render
  at set times).
- **Authorship:** studios may ask how material was made, and rules on
  owning AI-made work differ by country (not legal advice). The record of
  who made what supports Ness's authorship.
