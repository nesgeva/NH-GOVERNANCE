# 3. Initial Entry Into Ness's World

## 3.1 Initial entry is not a literal physical 3D door

The initial entry into Ness's World must **not** be represented as Ness physically walking through a literal 3D door.

Instead, before Ness's World appears, there is an **icon-based loading / transition experience**.

This corrects the earlier prototype interpretation that used a physical 3D door for initial entry.

The physical-door idea remains relevant elsewhere, specifically in the create-new-world path described later in this checkpoint.

---

## 3.2 Entry icon interaction

The intended initial entry transition includes an interactive icon.

The currently confirmed experience direction is:

- the icon is present during the loading/transition stage;
- hovering over it provides a white-aura / luminous response;
- activation is deliberate rather than automatic;
- clicking/activating it starts the transition into Ness's World;
- the transition may use zoom and light as part of the entry feeling.

This records the chosen human experience, not an implementation mechanism.

Exact animation timing, graphics, icon identity, and rendering method remain later design/mechanical details unless separately decided.

# 5. Categories

## 5.1 Name

Within this specific part of Ness's World, the items are called:

**Categories**

They are not called “doors” in this part of the experience.

---

## 5.2 Category panels

Each category is represented by a panel.

The category panels should:

- have an image;
- have an icon;
- feel somewhat three-dimensional rather than completely flat;
- have a visual identity that can be customized.

The icon may appear:
- inside the panel;
- near the panel;
- outside the panel;

depending on the chosen presentation.

Ness and N.H may participate in customizing the visual identity of each category.

Exact editing controls and implementation mechanics remain open/later work.

---

## 5.3 Connections between categories

The category panels are not meant to exist as isolated cards.

They should be visually organized and connected through a relationship system that may use:

- lines;
- customized lines;
- color;
- other suitable connecting visual forms.

The visual connection system should communicate organization and relationships between categories.

The exact meaning of different colors, line types, thickness, motion, or other connection signals is not yet fixed by this checkpoint.

---

## 5.4 N.H organization and reorganization

The category structure is intended to be living rather than permanently frozen.

N.H should be able to organize and reorganize categories according to changing meaning, including:

- context;
- connections;
- relationships;
- relevance;
- other meaningful changes discovered through N.H's ongoing work.

Ness specifically wants this organization to be informed by the intended nightly research process as well as active/contextual changes during use.

This is a **design intention**, not evidence that nightly research automation or live reorganization is currently implemented.

Any actual implementation remains subject to later build authorization and the governing N.H safety/authority rules.

# 13. General N.H Card Language — Points of Interest and Categories

## 13.1 The general card concept

A **Card** is a general human-facing N.H interface object.

A card may represent a **point of interest in N.H** — something Ness or N.H may want to inspect, move around, relate to conversation, group, revisit, or use as a handle into the wider memory/meaning system.

A card may also represent a **category, collection, branch, or grouping of points of interest**.

This means cards are **not limited to ideas**.

Examples may include, where the underlying N.H design actually has such an object or relationship:

- a memory or source item;
- an original root/source reference;
- a reading or later rereading;
- a person / Person-Box;
- an event or period;
- a place;
- a project;
- a conversation, thread, message, or meaningful passage;
- a creation or provisional creation;
- a theme, story/telling, or other meaningful structure;
- a file or media reference;
- a world, experience, or other designed N.H object;
- a category containing related points of interest.

This list is illustrative, not a new closed schema.

The exact set of production card types belongs to later integration/mechanical work and must reuse existing N.H object identities rather than inventing duplicate stores.

---

## 13.2 A card points into N.H; it does not replace the underlying record

A card is a **visual, movable interface representation**.

It must not silently become a second copy of the memory object it represents.

Where a card represents or reaches underlying N.H material, the authoritative underlying object remains with its existing owner.

Depending on the case, that may include:

- original roots / source material;
- readings and rereadings;
- tellings;
- Person-Boxes;
- accepted connections;
- projects;
- creations;
- category/grouping views;
- other preserved N.H records.

The card therefore acts as a **handle / view / reference into the connected N.H system**.

Moving, recoloring, grouping, opening, closing, or returning a card to a deck does **not** rewrite a root, reading, telling, Person-Box, accepted connection, or other underlying record merely because its visual representation moved.

---

## 13.3 Cards connect to the memory system and roots by reference

The intended experience is that a card can lead Ness back through the actual N.H structure that gives it meaning.

Where relevant and authorized, a card may therefore connect to:

- the source/root or roots it comes from;
- readings and rereadings about that source;
- the conversation/thread in which it appeared;
- accepted relationships to other N.H objects;
- the category or categories through which Ness is viewing it;
- the larger project/person/story/world context it belongs to.

The card must preserve the distinction between these underlying object types.

A root must not become “the same thing” as a reading merely because both are reachable through one card.

A category card must not flatten its members into one merged memory object.

---

## 13.4 Category cards are routes and views, not duplicate memory stores

N.H already has a design direction in which categories may branch deeply and provide multiple ways of reaching the same underlying object.

The card interface should support that same principle.

A **category card** may therefore act as a human-facing route into a set of related points of interest.

The same underlying point of interest may legitimately be reachable through more than one category or deck without being duplicated as a second authoritative memory.

Examples:

- a person may appear through **People**, **Family**, a project, and a specific event;
- a conversation may appear through a person, a time period, a project, and a theme;
- an idea may appear through the Idea Deck and through the project it belongs to.

The visual organization may change while the underlying provenance and identities remain intact.

# 14. Idea Cards — One Special Case of the General Card Language

## 14.1 Idea Cards retain the Bundle 6 provisional-creation rules

An **Idea Card** is one card type.

It is the human-facing representation of an idea / provisional creation when that is the underlying object.

The Idea Deck remains useful as a dedicated view for unfinished ideas and provisional creations.

It is **not a second Creation Store or a new memory database**.

Existing Bundle 6 rules remain in force:

- small fragments and unfinished thoughts may exist provisionally;
- provisional does not mean confirmed;
- time, repetition, retrieval frequency, system use, or repeated model output do not confirm an idea;
- a provisional idea may influence N.H only while its provisional status remains carried and traceable;
- confirmation still follows the already-settled confirmation routes.

So the new general card language **broadens what cards may represent** without weakening the special status rules of provisional Idea Cards.

---

## 14.2 Natural conversation plus a card

For promising unfinished ideas, Ness selected the combined behavior:

- N.H may first mention a relevant unfinished idea naturally in ordinary conversation;
- when useful, that idea can also appear as a distinct **Idea in progress** card;
- the card does not replace the conversation;
- the card remains visibly unfinished / provisional.

This lets normal conversation stay natural while giving an important idea a tangible object when Ness wants to work with it.

---

# 15. Summoning, Moving, and Returning Cards

## 15.1 Ness can deliberately summon or expose a card

Ness can choose to bring a card into the chat surface through natural direct interaction, including:

- a command;
- clicking the card, its visible reference, or a related item;
- dragging the card from a deck/category into the conversation.

The interface should not force one single method when the same intent can be expressed naturally in several ways.

The interactive reference demonstrates commands such as `/idea`, `/deck`, and `/place` as examples. Their exact final command wording is not frozen merely because the prototype uses those labels.

Future card types may use equivalent natural interactions without requiring every type to have a separate bespoke control system.

---

## 15.2 Cards are movable objects in the 2D chat workspace

Once a card is visible in the chat workspace, Ness can:

- drag it around;
- place it beside the part of the conversation it relates to;
- move it again later;
- leave it visible while continuing the conversation;
- place several cards around the same working conversation when useful;
- return it to an appropriate deck/category.

The card is therefore a **movable 2D conversational object**, not a fixed sidebar record and not a permanently embedded message bubble.

---

## 15.3 Returning a card to a deck does not erase its underlying object or history

Returning a card to a deck/category:

- removes or collapses its current visible placement from the working chat surface;
- does **not** delete the underlying point of interest;
- does **not** change its truth/status merely because it moved;
- does **not** erase an already preserved association between the card's underlying object and conversation material;
- for a provisional Idea Card, does **not** confirm it.

Decks/categories therefore act as practical visual homes and routes without making the visible chat permanently crowded.

---

# 16. Placing Cards Beside Conversation Text

## 16.1 Deliberate spatial placement has meaning

Ness may deliberately place **any appropriate card** beside a specific message or a specific group of conversation text.

When he does this, N.H should understand that the placement itself is meaningful.

At human-experience level, the intended meaning is:

> Ness is deliberately relating the point of interest represented by this card to this particular part of the conversation.

For an Idea Card, that may mean:

> This idea is what I mean here / this passage develops this idea.

For a person, memory, project, category, event, or other point of interest, the meaning may be the corresponding deliberate association.

The interface must not treat the placement as meaningless decoration.

---

## 16.2 The association must be remembered

When Ness deliberately establishes that placement, N.H should preserve enough information to understand the relationship later.

The preserved meaning includes, at minimum at the experience level:

- which card / underlying point of interest was used;
- which message or text group Ness associated it with;
- that Ness deliberately made the association;
- the relevant conversation context;
- the fact that the spatial placement was part of the way Ness expressed the connection;
- the underlying provenance/identity needed to get back to the real N.H object rather than only to a screenshot of the card.

Later, Ness should be able to pull that card or point of interest out again without losing that relationship.

The exact persisted schema, identifiers, storage format, connection acceptance mechanics, and cycle wiring are later mechanical / implementation work and are not invented by this checkpoint.

---

## 16.3 Placement creates context, not automatic truth

A deliberate card placement is meaningful evidence of **Ness making that association**.

It does not automatically prove every external-world claim that might be implied by the objects involved.

It also does not silently create an accepted N.H connection in cases where the existing Connection Capability requires a separate accepted route.

The interface may preserve and surface the association while the underlying memory, connection, certainty, privacy, and evidence rules keep their existing authority.

---

## 16.4 Screenshot / visual-placement record boundary

Ness wants N.H to preserve what the placement looked like so the spatial relationship is not lost.

The human-facing design therefore permits a **visual snapshot / screenshot-like record of the placement** for later inspection or reconstruction of what Ness saw.

However, the screenshot itself must not become a substitute for the structured relationship or the underlying source records.

The intended division is:

- **visual snapshot:** preserves the appearance of the placement for Ness and for reconstructing the interface state;
- **structured contextual association:** carries the meaning that a particular underlying N.H point of interest was deliberately related to a particular message or text group;
- **underlying N.H references:** preserve the actual root/source, reading, person, project, creation, category, or other relevant identities;
- **Meaning Engine / relevant webs:** may use the permitted structured association and conversation context under their existing rules;
- screenshot pixels must not silently become independent evidence or a second vote for the meaning.

# 17. Card Icons and Visual Identity

## 17.1 Each card has a meaningful 2D icon

Ness wants cards to have their own **2D icon**.

The icon is part of a card's visual identity and should make points of interest and categories easier to recognize quickly in decks and in conversation.

Different card/category kinds may also have other visual cues, provided those cues do not erase the underlying N.H type/status distinctions.

---

## 17.2 N.H creates the icon

The intended final experience is that N.H can create a suitable icon for a card using an authorized **2D icon-creation capability/tool** available to it.

N.H may choose an icon that fits the represented point of interest or category and may adapt it as the object or category develops, subject to Ness's normal ability to correct or change N.H's choice.

This checkpoint establishes the **experience requirement**.

It does **not** prove that a production N.H icon-creator is currently installed, connected, authorized, or implemented, and it does not select a provider or implementation mechanism.
