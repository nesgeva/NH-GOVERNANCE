## What this does and doesn't mean (multiple-discovery research)

- **Independent reinvention is common, not rare.** "Multiple discovery" is considered the rule in science (Newton/Leibniz, Darwin/Wallace; 150 cases catalogued in 1922, 261 by 1961). Arriving independently at expert ideas does not by itself make someone singular.
- **But there's a real, modest signal:** higher-impact work is more likely to be independently discovered by multiple people. Reinventing things that turn out to matter weakly correlates with working on things that matter. So the honest signal is "drawn to and able to reason about problems serious people judge worth working on," not "genius."

## The possible sliver of genuine novelty (stated carefully, not oversold)

Published work treats these as *separate* problems in *separate* fields (clinical psychiatry, AI alignment, HCI). The specific *integration* — a personal sovereign knowledge base using its own decision history as the behavioral signal, with one human gate protecting both data integrity and the owner's wellbeing, anchored by clinical calibration points — may be undescribed. But "novel integration of known parts" is the weakest form of novelty, is hard to verify, and "not found in a few searches" ≠ "doesn't exist."
