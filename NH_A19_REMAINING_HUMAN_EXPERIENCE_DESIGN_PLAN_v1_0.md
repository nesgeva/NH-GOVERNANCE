# N.H — A19 Remaining Human-Experience Design Plan

**Filename:** `NH_A19_REMAINING_HUMAN_EXPERIENCE_DESIGN_PLAN_v1_0.md`  
**Status:** `WORKING PLAN — NOT A DESIGN DECISION — NOT ACCEPTED — NOT ADOPTED — NOT AUTHORITATIVE — NOT IMPLEMENTED`  
**Owner of meaning and decisions:** Ness  
**Scope:** A19 only — the remaining human-facing experience design for Ness's World and Wonder.

---

## 0. Purpose

This file is a navigation and work-order plan for the remaining A19 human-experience design.

It does **not** settle any of the choices below.

It does **not** replace or modify:

- `NH_MASTER-20_CORRECTED_v10.md`;
- the current Design and Wiring Map;
- the accepted A17 Wonder-to-memory policy;
- the A19 Unreal Engine 5 runtime direction;
- any accepted package, historical file, or existing authority.

The A19 Unreal runtime direction already answers the major technical question of what runs Ness's World and Wonder: **Unreal Engine 5**.

The remaining work here is about what Ness actually experiences and controls when using Ness's World and Wonder.

---

## 1. Entering and leaving the experience

Design what actually happens visually and spatially when Ness enters Ness's World or Wonder, and how Ness returns.

This includes the experience of moving from ordinary N.H conversation into the world, moving between spaces where relevant, entering Wonder, and leaving or returning without confusing presentation, replay, reconstruction, or simulation.

---

## 2. Perspective and presence

Design how Ness experiences the world from his own point of view.

This includes:

- first-person experience;
- observer experience;
- adaptive use of both where appropriate;
- what Ness's own body, hands, or avatar mean on desktop;
- what Ness's own body, hands, or avatar mean in VR.

The goal is to decide the human experience, not to choose implementation details prematurely.

---

## 3. Interaction language

Design how Ness naturally communicates and acts inside the world.

This includes:

- voice;
- hands and gestures;
- mouse;
- keyboard;
- touch;
- combinations of those inputs;
- what happens when tracking is uncertain or signals conflict.

The interaction system should feel coherent rather than like unrelated control schemes placed beside one another.

---

## 4. Wonder visual truth-language

Design how the experience keeps different kinds of material unmistakably different.

The experience must let Ness understand when something is:

- replay;
- reconstruction;
- invented Wonder material;
- active simulation.

These distinctions must remain clear without making the experience ugly, distracting, or unnecessarily intrusive.

This plan does not change the accepted rule that Wonder is possibility/simulation material, not established fact.

---

## 5. Branching, time, saving, and revisiting as an experience

The A19 architecture already supports the underlying ideas of simulation branches, checkpoints, time control, and revisiting.

The remaining design work is the human-facing experience:

- how Ness sees branches;
- how Ness creates or changes branches;
- how Ness moves backward or forward;
- how saved points or preserved paths are presented;
- how Ness returns to an earlier point;
- how this remains understandable without turning Wonder into a technical control panel.

---

## 6. N.H's presence inside the world

Design how N.H appears and accompanies Ness inside Ness's World and Wonder.

Possible forms may include, where appropriate:

- voice only;
- a visible figure;
- light;
- a panel;
- an object;
- another contextual form.

The design must decide when N.H should use a form, when it should stay unobtrusive, and how adaptive its presence should be.

No single form is selected by this plan.

---

## 7. Camera / VR privacy and approval experience

The underlying N.H privacy, access, identity, and authority rules remain in force.

A19 still needs the actual user-facing experience that sits on top of those rules.

This includes:

- what Ness sees when camera or VR sensing is available;
- how permission or approval appears when it is genuinely needed;
- how Ness can understand what is currently active;
- how Ness can stop, change, or override it;
- how the experience stays protective without constantly interrupting normal use.

This section designs the experience only. It does not weaken or replace the governing privacy and authority rules.

---

## 8. Exact visual language

Design the overall visual grammar of Ness's World and Wonder.

This includes, where relevant:

- rooms and spaces;
- transitions;
- doors and movement between spaces;
- object types;
- visual representation of memory-linked material;
- mood or state representation;
- differences between ordinary world presentation and Wonder;
- how the system remains coherent as the world grows.

This is the visual language of the experience, not a requirement to force every space into one fixed style.

---

## 9. Working method

Work through these areas one at a time.

For each area:

1. Check the governing and relevant A19/Wonder design files first.
2. Separate what is already settled from what is genuinely open.
3. Ask Ness only for choices that change the actual meaning or experience.
4. Preserve Ness's answer together with its relevant context.
5. Do not stop after every answer to create a file.
6. At sensible checkpoints, preserve the accumulated A19 decisions in a new versioned A19 decision/design record.
7. Saving a decision does not by itself mean acceptance, adoption, Master/Map integration, or permission to build.

---

## 10. Completion meaning

This working plan is complete when all eight areas above have been examined and the genuinely open A19 human-experience decisions have either:

- been settled by Ness;
- been shown to be mechanically implied by already-settled design;
- been correctly left for a dependent later decision;
- or been correctly deferred to later mechanical/implementation work.

Completion of this plan alone does **not** make A19 `PACKAGE_COMPLETE`.

A19 closure still requires the normal N.H review, dependency, audit, acceptance, and package-closure process.
